## Objectives

- Review and refine the proposed taxonomy for Agentic AI use cases.
- Discuss how the taxonomy can support gap analysis and working group alignment.
- Identify areas where Agentic AI introduces new requirements beyond existing Internet protocols.
- Define next steps for socializing and improving the taxonomy.

---

## Discussion Summary

### 1. Purpose of the Taxonomy

The group reviewed the current taxonomy draft, which is intended to serve as a collaborative and evolving framework for organizing decentralized Agentic AI use cases.

**Primary goals:**
- Provide a structured way to categorize use cases.
- Support identification of protocol and architectural gaps.
- Help determine appropriate IETF Working Group ownership.
- Enable mapping between requirements and existing standards.

The taxonomy currently includes seven primary domains:

1. Transport
2. Security and Trust
3. Discovery and Identity
4. Coordination and Orchestration
5. Data and Context Management
6. Operations
7. Management

### 2. Use Case Documentation Framework

The group discussed the guidance provided for documenting use cases. The taxonomy aims to ensure use cases are complete, actionable, and technically grounded.

Recommended elements include:
- Clear scope definition
- Ecosystem boundaries
- Gap analysis
- Adoption and layering considerations
- Communication patterns and modes
- Performance requirements
- Safety requirements
- Trust boundaries
- Working Group ownership considerations
- Operational and deployment realism

Participants agreed that the taxonomy should function as both a classification tool and a checklist for use case authors.

### 3. Highlighting What Is New in Agentic AI

A recurring theme was the importance of explicitly identifying what differentiates Agentic AI systems from existing distributed systems.

Key observations included:
- Protocol designers need clearer visibility into genuinely novel requirements.
- Emerging characteristics such as multimodal interactions and new trust relationships should be emphasized.
- The taxonomy should help distinguish between existing protocol capabilities, extensions of existing capabilities, and completely new requirements.

### 4. Gap Analysis and Coverage Mapping

The discussion emphasized the value of embedding gap identification directly within the taxonomy.

Suggested capabilities:
- Label requirements as covered by existing standards, partially covered, or not currently addressed.
- Map gaps to relevant IETF Working Groups.
- Identify areas requiring new protocol work.

### 5. Discovery, Identity, and Trust Challenges

A significant discussion focused on agent discovery and identity management.

**Key challenges identified:**
- Traditional name-based discovery mechanisms may be insufficient for Agentic AI scenarios.
- Agent interactions frequently begin with capability or task discovery rather than known identifiers.
- Durable and accountable identifiers are needed.
- Lifecycle management and entity-level accountability remain important gaps.

### 6. Alignment with Existing BOFs and Working Groups

Participants highlighted the importance of ensuring that the taxonomy creates tangible value for existing Birds of a Feather (BOF) efforts and current or future IETF Working Groups.

Key points included:
- The taxonomy should leverage use cases, requirements, and material already produced by relevant BOFs and Working Groups.
- Existing work should be incorporated rather than duplicated.
- The taxonomy should evolve to include the key classes and subclasses necessary to classify and map work produced across these communities.
- Success will depend on making the taxonomy directly useful to BOFs and WGs for organizing requirements, identifying gaps, and guiding future standardization work.

### 7. Collaborative Development Model

The group reaffirmed that the taxonomy should remain community-driven and easy to evolve.

---

## Decisions

- Continue evolving the taxonomy as a collaborative working document.
- Use the taxonomy as both a classification framework and a checklist for documenting Agentic AI use cases.
- Strengthen visibility of novel Agentic AI requirements and protocol gaps.
- Improve mechanisms for mapping requirements to existing standards and Working Groups.
- Ensure the taxonomy aligns with and incorporates relevant outputs from existing BOFs and Working Groups.

## Key Takeaway

The meeting concluded that the taxonomy should become a practical community tool that helps organize Agentic AI use cases, identify protocol gaps, and guide standardization efforts. Particular emphasis should be placed on ensuring alignment with existing BOF and Working Group efforts so that the taxonomy delivers immediate value across the IETF ecosystem.
